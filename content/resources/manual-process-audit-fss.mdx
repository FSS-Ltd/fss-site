## Which process should we audit first?

Choose one repeated workflow with a known owner. Record what actually happens, including spreadsheet copies, inbox checks and informal handoffs. The existing PDF provides the audit framework; the examples below help charity and faith teams apply it.

## What should we record?

For each step, write down the trigger, person responsible, system used, output and next recipient. Count tasks over a representative period and record active work separately from waiting time. Mark estimates clearly and include exceptions such as duplicate or incomplete requests.

An illustrative charity referral audit might follow a request from a shared inbox through eligibility review to assignment. Record how often staff ask for missing information and who decides whether a referral can proceed. Do not place personal referral details in the workbook.

An illustrative church volunteer audit might follow availability collection, rota preparation, substitutions and confirmation. Check how changes reach occasional volunteers and how the coordinator knows they were received.

## How do we rank the findings?

Compare frequency, effort, consequences of a mistake and the practicality of a change. A rare but serious access mistake should not disappear behind a high-volume administrative task. Discuss sensitive workflows with the accountable person; the audit is not a legal or compliance certification.

Keep observed evidence beside each proposed improvement. If a handoff lacks an owner, clarify responsibility before automating it. If copying between tools creates repeated work, investigate whether supported interfaces exist. The [workflow automation service](/services/workflow-automation) explains the scope FSS can assess.

## How do we test an improvement?

Write one acceptance task using fictional records: an authorised coordinator can assign a request, see its next action and find its history. Agree a baseline and a later review period. Time released is capacity that can be used elsewhere; it is not automatically money saved.

Compare the options and ongoing responsibility in the [Software Investment Framework](/resources/software-investment-framework). Use the [Software Project Readiness Kit](/resources/software-project-readiness-kit) when the evidence is clear enough to prepare a supplier brief.

## What should we do next?

Select one change, give it an owner and define what result would justify continuing. A charity or faith team can use the [sector guidance](/sectors/charities-faith-organisations) to connect the finding with a suitable kind of software work.
